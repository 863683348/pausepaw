PausePaw vs Cold Turkey Blocker：硬核拦截的真实差别

2026-10-04

Cold Turkey 是 Windows 上最狠的拦截器，PausePaw 是你愿意一直装着的那个。这篇讲清各自赢在哪，以及五分钟内怎么选。

会搜 pausepaw vs cold turkey 的人，通常已经试过温和的办法了。定时提醒、用量统计、善意提示——它们都对，也都失败在同一个地方：你能在想要退出的那一刻退出。Cold Turkey Blocker 的存在就是为了堵死这个出口，而 PausePaw 走的是另一条路。这篇不打算说谁更好，只讲清楚各自赢在哪，以及你该装哪一个。

Cold Turkey 做到而别人做不到的事

Cold Turkey 是 Windows 上最硬的一档拦截器，这一点没什么可争的。它的 Frozen Turkey 模式一旦启动，你不能提前结束：没有暂停按钮，没有再来五分钟这一项，卸载重装也不行。它的设计前提是你会跟自己谈判，所以它干脆不给你开口的机会。

另外两块也很扎实。一是按网站、按应用、按关键词分别设规则，粒度足够细；二是它只跑在你装了它的那台机器上，不上传数据，也不要求注册账号。对在意隐私的人来说，这是个实打实的优点。

  • 硬锁：启动之后没有提前结束的入口，这就是它最核心的价值。
  • 规则粒度：可以只拦某个域名下的某个路径，也可以只拦某一个应用。
  • 本地运行：不依赖账号，数据留在你自己的机器上。

PausePaw 作为 cold turkey alternative 赢在哪

硬锁的问题不在它拦不住你，而在你会开始躲它。多数人第一次用 Frozen Turkey 的那一周很有效，第二周开始绕：换浏览器、改 hosts、用另一台设备。绕不过去的人留下了，绕得过去的人通常在两周后就不再打开它了。

PausePaw 的判断正好相反：一个让你心里抵触的工具撑不过一个月。所以它把力气花在两个地方——规则挂在你正在做的事上，而不是挂在一个固定钟点上；被打断的时候先给一次提醒，而不是立刻立一堵墙。你在赶一个截止，PausePaw 拦的是那四个把你拖住的站点，不是整台机器。

  • 绑定行为：规则跟着你现在在做什么走，不跟着时间表走。
  • 提醒优先：先打断，再拦截，给判断力留一个能插进来的缝隙。
  • 起步成本：免费档就能用，不需要先说服自己买一年订阅。

两台机器上的第三天

差别在第三天变得清楚。用硬锁的人，第三天的体验是这个拦截不再像一个需要你每天早上重新做的决定，它变成了当天就在那里的一堵墙。这恰恰是有些人需要的。另一些人的第三天整段都在找出口，而找到出口的人后来往往就不再续费了。

用 PausePaw 的人，第三天会注意到那个暂停是不是真的打断了你。打断了，习惯就留下来；没打断，就把规则收紧一格。它的失败方式更软，要求的承诺也更轻。

五分钟内怎么选

别比功能表，比你自己最糟的那一小时：它发生在哪台机器上、你当时在躲什么、你过去是怎么把每一个温和版本取消掉的。这三个答案出来的那一刻，选择基本就定了。

  • 选 Cold Turkey：你只在一台 Windows 电脑上出问题，而且你已经证明自己会取消任何能取消的拦截。
  • 选 PausePaw：你要的是能一直装着的执行力，希望拦截跟着你手头的事走，并且想先不花钱试。
  • 两个都装：很少需要。除非你确实有一个具体缺口，比如夜里要把手机也一起拦掉。

常见问题

  • Cold Turkey 真的绕不过去吗？不完全。它是这个品类里最难绕的，但任何声称绝对绕不过的工具都在夸大。有意义的问题是绕过去要花多大代价。
  • PausePaw 能做硬锁吗？能做定时拦截，但刻意没有做成不可撤销。如果你的结论是我必须被锁死，那 Cold Turkey 更合适。
  • 两个一起用会不会打架？一般不会，但两套规则叠在一起很难判断是哪一套起了作用。先用一套跑两周。
  • 免费版够用吗？Cold Turkey 有免费档，够基础拦截；PausePaw 免费档覆盖基本规则和提醒。真正的差别在你要多少条规则。

选一个，装上，让它陪你最糟的那一小时跑两周。功能表没法告诉你一个月后你还在用哪一个。PausePaw 在首页就能开始设置，更多对比在博客，比如和 Freedom 的那篇，以及一步到位还是循序渐进。

Anyone searching pausepaw vs cold turkey has usually already tried the gentle version. Timers, usage reports, polite nudges. They all work, and they all fail in the same place: you can quit at the moment you want to quit. Cold Turkey Blocker exists to close that exit, and PausePaw takes a different road. This is not about which one is better. It is about where each one wins, and which one you should install.

What Cold Turkey does that nothing else matches

Cold Turkey is the hardest blocker on Windows, and that is not really up for debate. Once Frozen Turkey is running, you cannot end it early. No pause button, no five more minutes, and reinstalling does not help. The design assumes you will negotiate with yourself, so it removes your side of the negotiation.

Two other pieces are solid. Rules can be set per site, per app, or per keyword, which is fine-grained enough for most people. And it runs only on the machine where you installed it, with no account and no data leaving your disk. If you care about privacy, that counts for something.

  • Hard lock: no way out once it starts. That is the whole product.
  • Rule depth: block one path under one domain, or one application, without touching the rest.
  • Local only: no account needed, nothing uploaded.

Where PausePaw wins as a cold turkey alternative

The problem with a hard lock is not that it fails to stop you. It is that you start avoiding it. Week one with Frozen Turkey works for most people. By week two they are routing around it: a different browser, a hosts edit, another device. The people who cannot get around it keep it. The people who can usually stop opening it by week three.

PausePaw starts from the opposite bet: a tool you resent does not survive a month. So the effort goes into two things instead. Rules attach to what you are doing rather than to a fixed hour on the clock, and the interruption arrives as a reminder before it becomes a wall. If you are working against a deadline, PausePaw blocks the four sites that pull you off it, not the whole machine.

  • Bound to behaviour: rules follow the task in front of you, not a timetable.
  • Reminder first: interrupt, then block, leaving a gap your judgement can use.
  • Cost to start: the free tier is usable, so you do not have to commit to a year before testing it.

Day three on each one

The split shows up on day three. With a hard lock, the block stops being a decision you remake each morning and becomes a wall that is simply there. Some people need exactly that. Others spend all of day three looking for a way out, and the ones who find one tend not to renew.

With PausePaw, day three is when you notice whether the pause actually interrupts you. If it does, the habit sticks. If it does not, you tighten one rule. It fails more softly, and it asks for less commitment.

How to pick in five minutes

Skip the feature table and look at your worst hour instead. Which machine it happens on, what you are avoiding at the time, and how you have cancelled every softer version before. Once you have those three answers, the choice is mostly made.

  • Pick Cold Turkey if: the problem lives on one Windows machine, and you have already proved you will cancel any block that can be cancelled.
  • Pick PausePaw if: you want enforcement you will keep installed, rules that follow the task in front of you, and a free start.
  • Install both if: rarely. Only when a specific gap remains, such as covering your phone at night.

FAQ

  • Can Cold Turkey actually be bypassed? Not completely, though it is the hardest in this category. Any app claiming it cannot be bypassed at all is overstating it. The useful question is how much friction bypassing costs.
  • Does PausePaw do hard locking? It blocks on a schedule but stops short of making a block irrevocable. If your conclusion is that you must be locked in, Cold Turkey is the better fit.
  • Do the two conflict if installed together? Usually not, but two overlapping rule sets make it hard to tell which one did the work. Run one for two weeks first.
  • Is the free version enough? Cold Turkey has a free tier covering basic blocking, and the PausePaw free tier covers base rules and reminders. The gap shows up in how many rules you need.
