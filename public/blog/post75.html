开学季青少年手机规则：撑到十月的那几条

2026-10-20

九月定的规则，到十月中旬就塌了。这篇给三条撑得住的青少年手机规则，以及值得写进合约的四条条款。

开学季定下的手机规则，通常在十月中旬前后悄悄失效。看过足够多家庭之后，我觉得问题不在孩子不听话：规则是照着九月写的，那时候作业轻、刚开学还有点新鲜劲。到了十月，作业翻倍，规则却没跟着动。

\n

所以这篇写给十月的青少年手机规则：哪些条款真能撑住，哪些每天晚上都要重谈，以及值得落到纸面上的四条。

\n

为什么九月的规则到十月就塌了

\n

九月是有余量的。老师还在复习，社团没开，谁都没有真正的截止日期。这时候「写完作业玩一小时」看着很容易，头两周也确实印证了这一点。

\n

十月把这点余量拿走了。考试叠上来，小组作业出现，手机也不再只是玩具：项目讨论在群聊里，课表在上面，校车到哪了也要看它。一条不区分这两种用途的规则，会每个晚上都被打破，而家长最后争的是一个具体消息，不是规则本身。

\n

三条撑得过十月的青少年手机规则

\n
    \n
  • 把学习用途和刷屏分开。群聊、课表、作业类 app 放在限额之外；带无限信息流的东西放进限额里。这一条能结束大部分晚上的争吵，因为「可是我要用它做项目」的答案变成了可以。
    • \n
  • 关一个时段，不要设每日总量。每日总量每天晚上都要重谈一次；关掉一个时段，比如你能管住的每台设备 22:00 到 07:00，只需要决定一次，之后它自己跑。总量招来讨价还价，时段不招。
    • \n
  • 先写后果，再写规则。时段被打破的时候会发生什么。不是惩罚，是后果：第二天晚上手机睡在厨房。选什么都可以，前提是它小到你在一个累得不想说话的周二晚上真的会执行。
    • \n
\n

前六周通常会怎么走

\n
周次会变的规则需要什么
1还没变化，新鲜劲撑着一个时段，写下来
2作业来了把学习用途挪出限额
3第一次真正的顶撞你早就写好的那个后果
4群聊把界限又推远了群聊豁免，信息流留在里面
5睡眠开始移动守住时段，不要把口子放大
6规则不再被提起什么都不用做，别去动它
\n

第三周是多数家庭放弃的地方，也是决定后面几周的一周。顶撞不是规则失败的信号，是规则开始真正有代价的信号。

\n

合约里该写哪几条

\n

四条：时段、豁免清单、后果、还有一个复核日期。复核日期比家长想象的更重要：写了日期的规则可以改，双方都不丢面子；没写日期的规则，第一次需要调整的时候就会变成一场角力。

\n

控制在一页以内。超过一页的合约，两边都读不过第一行。

\n

常见问题

\n
    \n
  • 孩子写作业要用手机，这不就超了吗？不超，说明限额划错了地方。把学习工具挪出去，把信息流放进来。
    • \n
  • 每日总量和关闭时段哪个更好？时段。总量每天晚上都要重新谈一次，而一条每天都要重谈的规则不算规则。
    • \n
  • 已经十月了，我们什么都没定过。今晚先关一个时段。起步晚在这里不花代价，会失败的只有一上来就定六条的那一种。
    • \n
  • 要不要看他的屏幕时间报告？一周一次，一起看，念出声。一个人看，数字就变成了证据，对话就变成了审问。
    • \n

Back-to-school screen time rules get written in the first week of September and quietly stop working somewhere around the middle of October. I have watched it happen in enough households to think the problem is not teenage defiance. The rules were written for September, when homework was light and the new term still had some novelty in it. By mid-October the workload has doubled and the rules have not moved.

\n

So this is a set of teen back to school phone rules written for October instead. Which clauses hold, which ones get renegotiated every night, and the four worth putting on paper.

\n

Why September rules collapse by October

\n

September has slack. Teachers are still revising, clubs have not started, and nobody has a real deadline yet. A rule like one hour after homework looks easy then, and the first two weeks confirm it.

\n

October removes that slack. Tests stack up, group projects appear, and the phone stops being only a toy: it holds the group chat where the project is organised, the timetable, the bus tracker. A rule that does not separate those two kinds of use gets broken every night, and the parent ends up arguing about one specific message instead of about the rule.

\n

Three teen back to school phone rules that survive October

\n
    \n
  • Split school use from scroll use. Group chats, timetables and homework apps sit outside the limit. Anything with an infinite feed sits inside it. This one change ends most nightly arguments, because the answer to "but I need it for the project" becomes yes.
    • \n
  • Close a window, do not set a daily total. A total has to be negotiated every evening. A closed window, say 22:00 to 07:00 on every device you control, gets decided once and then runs on its own. Totals invite bargaining, windows do not.
    • \n
  • Write the consequence before the rule. What happens the night the window gets broken. Not a punishment, a consequence: the phone sleeps in the kitchen the next night. Whatever you pick has to be small enough that you will still carry it out on a Tuesday when you are too tired to argue.
    • \n
\n

What the first six weeks usually look like

\n
WeekWhat changesWhat the rule needs
1Nothing yet, novelty carries itOne window, written down
2Homework arrivesSchool use moved out of the limit
3The first real pushbackThe consequence you already wrote
4Group chats move the line againExempt the chat, keep the feed inside
5Sleep starts shiftingHold the window, do not widen it
6The rule stops coming upNothing. Leave it alone.
\n

Week three is where most families give up, and it is the week that decides the ones after it. Pushback is not the sign that a rule failed. It is the sign that the rule started costing something.

\n

What to write into the contract

\n

Four clauses: the window, the exempt list, the consequence, and a review date. The review date matters more than parents expect. A rule with a date on it can be changed without anybody losing face, and a rule without one turns into a power struggle the first time it needs adjusting.

\n

Keep it to a single page. Contracts longer than that do not get read past the first line, by either side.

\n

FAQ

\n
    \n
  • My teen needs the phone for homework. Does that break the limit? No, it means the limit was drawn in the wrong place. Move the school tools out of the count and put the feeds in.
    • \n
  • Is a daily total or a closed window better? A window. Totals get renegotiated every evening, and a rule you renegotiate daily is not a rule.
    • \n
  • It is October and we never set anything up. Close one window tonight. Starting late costs nothing here, and the only version that fails is the one that begins with six rules.
    • \n
  • Should I check their screen time report? Once a week, together, out loud. Read it alone and the number turns into evidence while the conversation turns into an interrogation.
    • \n
