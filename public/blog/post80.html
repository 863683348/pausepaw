屏幕眼疲劳：20-20-20 法则到底管不管用

2026-10-26

20-20-20 法则是屏幕眼疲劳被重复最多、也最常被放弃的一条建议。它对干眼和调焦疲劳做了什么，间隔为什么是 20 分钟，以及怎么让提醒活过一整个忙周。

屏幕眼疲劳有个听起来很医学的名字，成因却一点都不神秘。20-20-20 法大概是这类建议里被重复得最多的一条：每 20 分钟，看 20 英尺（约 6 米）外的东西 20 秒。多数人听完试一下午，到周四就放弃了。这篇讲这条法则实际做了什么、屏幕眼疲劳是怎么来的，以及怎么让提醒活过一个忙周。

屏幕眼疲劳到底是什么

屏幕眼疲劳（digital eye strain）是一组症状，不是一个诊断：眼睛干、眼眶后面钝钝地疼、眨一下眼才重新变清楚的模糊，以及下午才来的头痛。这些都不会伤害眼睛。这个区分很重要——担心屏幕在慢慢毁掉视力并没有证据支持，而相信它的人往往会跳过那些朴素但有用的做法。

大部分不适来自两个机制。一是眨眼频率：聊天时人一分钟眨 15 到 20 次，盯着屏幕读东西时大约只剩三分之一。眨得少，泪膜就破裂，眼球表面变干。二是调焦肌肉：连续几小时把焦点固定在一个距离上，这块肌肉一直收缩，跟别的肌肉保持一个姿势太久一样会酸。

20-20-20 法则做了什么

这条法则用一个动作处理上面两件事。看大约 20 英尺外的东西时，调焦肌肉接近放松状态；保持 20 秒，泪膜有时间重新铺满眼球表面。间隔定在 20 分钟，是因为近距离用眼开始几分钟内眨眼频率就会明显下降，并且在整段时间里一直保持低位。

20 英尺约等于 6 米。实际上多数房间最远的那面墙就够了，看向窗外更好。距离不必精确，关键是眼睛不再维持近距调焦。

为什么这条法则总被忘掉

法则本身很简单，出问题的不是法则。是没有东西告诉你 20 分钟已经过去了。盯着屏幕时时间会被压缩，你本来打算抬头的那一刻，通常正好是还差一段就看完的那一刻。

依赖你自己看表的提醒会失败。打断工作的提醒失败的方式不一样——人们会把它关掉。真正留下来的是绑在你已经在做的事上的提示，或者一个自己出现、又短到让你愿意照做的提示。

在办公桌前怎么记住它

  • 跟一件你本来就在做的事绑在一起。每记完一页笔记，或者每次构建跑完，抬头看一次。绑定比一个你得记得去启动的计时器管用。
  • 把提示从脑子里挪出去。在屏幕边上、和视线齐平的位置贴张便签，比手机提醒好用，因为便签不要求你做决定。
  • 让浏览器来开这个头。超过你设的时间后，PausePaw 会让下一个页面延迟十秒加载，而十秒的空白正好是这条法则需要的窗口。在pause-paw.shop设置。
  • 停顿时刻意眨眼。望向房间另一头的时候慢眨三下。看起来傻，对干眼的帮助比看远本身还大。
  • 先改房间，再买东西。屏幕略低于视线，距离大约一臂，不要有灯直接照在上面。

这条法则治不了的

有三件事常被算到屏幕头上，但看远处解决不了：眼镜度数没配准引起的头痛、由闪烁或眩光诱发的偏头痛、以及背后有医学病因的干眼。如果规律休息一整周后症状还在，或者你看到灯光周围有光晕、是疼而不是钝痛，去看验光师。这条法则是个习惯，不是一场需要通过的考试。

从一个间隔开始

先做两小时那版：站起来，走到最远的窗户前，待三十秒。明天做三次。等习惯站稳了，20 分钟那版更好，但两小时那版才是你三月还在做的那个。

Eye strain from screens has a clinical-sounding name and an unremarkable cause. The 20-20-20 rule is the most repeated piece of advice for it: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. Most people hear it, try it for an afternoon, and have dropped it by Thursday. This covers what the rule actually does, where screen eye strain comes from in the first place, and how to get the reminder to survive a busy week.

What screen eye strain actually is

Screen eye strain, also called digital eye strain, is a cluster of symptoms rather than a diagnosis. Dry eyes, a dull ache behind them, blurred vision that clears when you blink, and a headache that shows up in the late afternoon. None of it damages the eye. That distinction matters, because the fear that screens slowly ruin your eyesight is not supported by the evidence, and believing it makes people skip the plain fixes that do work.

Two mechanisms produce most of the discomfort. The first is blink rate. People blink roughly 15 to 20 times a minute in conversation and about a third of that while reading a screen. Fewer blinks means the tear film breaks up and the surface of the eye dries out. The second is the focusing muscle. Holding focus at one fixed distance for hours keeps a single muscle contracted, and it gets sore the way any muscle held in one position gets sore.

How the 20-20-20 rule works

The rule addresses both mechanisms with one action. Looking at something about 20 feet away relaxes the focusing muscle, because at that distance it is close to rest. Holding it for 20 seconds gives the tear film time to spread back across the eye. The 20-minute interval is there because blink rate drops measurably within minutes of starting close work and stays down for the rest of the session.

20 feet is about six metres. In practice the far wall of most rooms is close enough, and a window is better. The distance does not have to be exact. What matters is that your eyes stop holding a near focus.

Why the 20-20-20 rule gets forgotten

The rule is simple and the rule is not the problem. Nothing tells you when 20 minutes have passed. Attention on a screen compresses time, and the moment you meant to look up is usually the moment you are one paragraph from finishing something.

Reminders that depend on you checking the clock fail. Reminders that interrupt work fail differently, because people switch them off. What survives is a cue attached to something you already do, or one that arrives on its own and is short enough that obeying it costs nothing.

How to remember the 20-20-20 rule at a desk

  • Pair it with something you already do. Every time you finish a page of notes, or every time a build finishes, look up. Pairing beats a timer you have to remember to start.
  • Move the cue out of your head. A note at eye level beside the screen works better than a phone alert, because a note does not ask you to make a decision.
  • Let the browser open the gap. After your limit, PausePaw delays the next page by ten seconds, and ten seconds of nothing is exactly the window this rule needs. Set it at pause-paw.shop.
  • Blink on purpose during the pause. Three slow blinks while you look across the room. It looks silly and it does more for dryness than the distance does.
  • Fix the room before buying anything. Screen slightly below eye level, about an arm away, no lamp shining straight into it.

What the 20-20-20 rule does not fix

Three things get blamed on screens and are not solved by looking away. Headaches from an outdated prescription, migraines triggered by flicker or glare, and dry eye with a medical cause behind it. If symptoms hold steady after a week of regular breaks, or if you see halos around lights and feel pain rather than a dull ache, see an optometrist. The rule is a habit, not a test you pass.

Start with one interval

Take the two-hour version first: stand up, walk to the furthest window, stay there for thirty seconds. Do it three times tomorrow. The 20-minute version is better once the habit exists, but the two-hour version is the one people are still doing in March.
