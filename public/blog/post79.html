手机使用与睡眠：60 分钟法则

2026-10-25

对多数人来说，60 分钟比 90 分钟更站得住。这篇讲这条线为什么定在这里，以及多出来的那一小时该怎么用。

关于手机使用与睡眠的建议，最后通常只剩一个数字：睡前半小时放下手机、一小时、两小时。数字后面很少给理由，于是多数人试一晚，失败，然后断定这条规则是写给更有自制力的人的。60 分钟这个版本能站住，是因为它算的是「这一小时用来干什么」，而不是靠意志力硬扛。

睡前那一小时，身体需要做成两件事，而屏幕会同时干扰这两件。一是身体需要一个无聊的收尾窗口，二是大脑需要一个有尽头的窗口。信息流在两项上都不过关：它一直在生产新材料，每一条都带着一点情绪。它天然没有该停下的地方，这正是凌晨一点你会说出「再看五分钟」的原因。

为什么是 60 分钟，不是 90

90 分钟是多数睡眠文章推荐的版本，理论上也更好。实际上它要求你交出整个晚饭后的时段，而那是一天里人们最不愿意让出去的部分。第二天晚上遵从度就掉了，而一条被你放弃的规则，比一条更短但你守得住的规则更糟。

60 分钟刚好能清掉真正消耗睡眠的那两样。它长到足够让亮屏带来的清醒感退下去，也长到让你脑袋碰到枕头时，最后读到的东西不再是还在转的那一条。它同时短到可以守住：你让出一集剧，或者一轮消息，不是让出整个晚上。

睡前那一小时身体在做什么

时间段身体在做什么什么会打断它
睡前 60 到 30 分钟体温开始下降，清醒度回落贴脸的亮光，高唤醒内容
睡前 30 到 10 分钟睡眠压力累积，注意力收窄任何需要你做决定的东西，包括「下一集看什么」
最后 10 分钟过渡，通常已经关灯躺下再看一眼，而这一眼稳定地要价二十分钟

第三行是损失最集中的地方。那一次临睡前的查看本身不长，但它把倒计时重置了。如果只能改一处，改这一处。

怎么让睡前手机规则守得住

  • 定钟点，不要定倒计时。「22:30」比「睡前一小时」好用，因为倒计时从你决定开始的那一刻才走，而那个决定会一直往后挪。
  • 充电器放到够不着的地方。距离是最便宜的干预手段，代价只是难受两晚。
  • 替换，不要清空。纸质书、洗澡、拉伸十分钟。习惯不会因为被禁止就消失，它是在那个位置被别的东西占住之后才消失的。
  • 让第一次打开等一等。过了你定的时间，PausePaw 会让第一条 feed 延迟十秒加载。十秒通常够想起来你已经困了。

60 分钟还不够的时候

如果你入睡没问题，却在凌晨三点醒来伸手摸手机，那问题不在截止时间上。那是醒来的习惯，需要另一种修法：充电器放在必须站起来才够得到的地方，并且一直放到早上。

如果真的离不开屏幕才能睡着，从 20 分钟开始，每周加十分钟。20 分钟能守住的规矩，比 60 分钟守不住的规矩有用得多。

从最后十分钟开始

手机使用与睡眠的习惯一次只能改一个，而最后十分钟是代价最低的那个。今晚定一个钟点，把充电器放到另一个房间，然后留一周不改。

Phone use sleep advice usually arrives as a single number: put the phone down 30 minutes before bed, or an hour, or two hours. The number rarely comes with a reason, so people try it for one night, fail, and decide the rule was written for somebody with more discipline. The 60-minute version survives because it is built on what the hour is for, not on willpower.

Two things need to happen in the hour before you sleep, and a screen interferes with both. Your body needs the wind-down window to be boring, and your brain needs it to be finite. A scrolling feed fails both tests at once: it keeps producing new material, and each piece carries a small emotional charge. There is no natural place to stop, which is exactly why the phrase just five more minutes gets said out loud at 1 a.m.

Why 60 minutes and not 90

The 90-minute version is the one most sleep writers recommend, and in theory it is better. In practice it asks you to give up the entire after-dinner block, which is the part of the evening people are least willing to surrender. Compliance drops on night two, and a rule you drop is worse than a shorter rule you keep.

Sixty minutes clears the two things that actually cost you sleep. It is long enough for the alertness a bright screen produces to fade, and long enough that the last thing you read is not the thing you are still working on when your head hits the pillow. It is also short enough to defend: you give up one episode, or one round of messages, not the evening.

What the hour before bed is doing

WindowWhat the body is doingWhat breaks it
60 to 30 minutes before bedBody temperature starts dropping, alertness fallsBright light close to the face, high-arousal content
30 to 10 minutesSleep pressure builds, attention narrowsAnything that asks for a decision, including what to watch next
Last 10 minutesTransition, usually in bed with the light offOne more check, which reliably costs twenty minutes

The third row is where most of the damage happens. The late check is not long, but it resets the countdown. If you only fix one thing, fix that one.

Making the phone before sleep rule hold

  • Pick a clock time, not a countdown. 22:30 works better than an hour before I sleep, because a countdown starts when you decide to start it, and that decision keeps moving.
  • Move the charger out of reach. Distance is the cheapest intervention there is. It costs two uncomfortable nights.
  • Replace the scroll, do not remove it. A paperback, a shower, ten minutes of stretching. The habit does not vanish because you banned it; it vanishes when something else occupies the slot.
  • Let the first tap wait. After your cutoff, PausePaw delays the first feed load by ten seconds. Ten seconds is usually long enough to remember that you were tired.

When 60 minutes is not enough

If you fall asleep fine but wake at 3 a.m. and reach for the phone, the cutoff is not the problem. That is a waking habit, and it needs a different fix: the phone charges somewhere you have to stand up to reach, and it stays there until morning.

If you genuinely cannot fall asleep without a screen, start at 20 minutes and add ten a week. A rule you follow at 20 minutes does more for you than one you abandon at 60.

Start with the last ten minutes

Phone use sleep habits change one at a time, and the last ten minutes are the cheapest one to change. Pick a clock time tonight, put the charger in another room, and leave it there for a week before you adjust anything.
