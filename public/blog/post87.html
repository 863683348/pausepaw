iOS 屏幕时间：2026 完整指南

2026-11-02

每一项设置都讲清楚，再补上多数指南略过的 3 个隐藏项。

每台 iPhone 上都预装了一整套屏幕时间控制，但几乎没有人用到两项以上。原因不是懒。这些设置分散在四个菜单里，其中三项的措辞和实际作用对不上，而大家最想找的那个功能（硬性屏蔽）被放在密码界面后面。

这是一份针对 2026 年版本的 ios 屏幕时间指南，按你实际动手的顺序写。凡是名字有误导性的设置，我会直接标出来。

这些控制到底在哪

打开「设置」，点「屏幕使用时间」。下面讲的所有东西都挂在这一屏上。动手之前先认清楚这四个分类：

设置项它实际做什么值得设吗
停用时间按时间表屏蔽所有 App，白名单里的除外值得，四项里最有力的一项
App 限额按天给一个分类或单个 App 设上限值得，但先从分类开始
通信限额控制停用时间里谁能联系到你用了停用时间才需要
内容与隐私年龄分级、购买限制、网页过滤主要给孩子设备用

停用时间，以及没人找得到的白名单

停用时间是所有设置里最贴近大家对「屏幕时间控制」预期的那一项。在你设定的时段内，所有 App 都会变灰，只有你明确允许的那些还能用。

多数指南跳过的是白名单的位置。它不在停用时间那一屏。去「屏幕使用时间」→「始终允许」，把需要保持可用的 App 选上。这一步要在设时段之前做：第一次停用时间里没有地图、钱包和验证码 App，体验差到大多数人会直接把整个功能关掉，然后再也不回来。

还有一点，如果这部手机是孩子的：停用时间要在「家人共享」里设，不要在设备上设。在设备上设的时间表，拿着手机的人点一下就能给自己放行，这正是需要分开设置的原因。

App 限额，以及为什么分类比单个 App 更好用

App 限额是最容易用力过猛的地方。给某个社交 App 设三十分钟，下午两点就撞线，然后接下来一周都在点「忽略限额」。同一个限额被忽略三次之后，那个数字就不再有任何意义了。

第一次做，分类更好用。给「社交」整体设一个限额，而不是给四个 App 各设一个，等于你先决定今天想滑多久，手机不关心你滑的是哪一个。这更接近这个习惯真实的运作方式。

要紧的是「达到限额后阻止使用」这个开关。不开它，撞线之后会出现一个灰色界面，上面有个让你继续的按钮。开了它，这个 App 就停到第二天，或者等有人输入密码。

多数指南略过的三个设置

  • 屏幕距离。在「屏幕使用时间」里有一项，用原深感摄像头判断手机是不是被长时间拿在离脸约 30 厘米以内的地方。它看起来像个噱头，但它是整台手机上唯一一个针对姿势问题、而不是时长问题的控制项。
  • 通知的送达时机。多数设置指南讲到通知数量就停了。真正更大的杠杆是通知什么时候到，而这个开关是按 App 分别设的，不在全局里，所以没人找得到。
  • 跨设备共享。默认是开的，意味着 iPad 或 Mac 的使用会算进同一份限额。想要一个总数时很有用；如果你的工作 iPad 在给个人手机的总量灌水，就把它关掉。

密码该放在哪一步

设一个屏幕使用时间密码，不要和开机密码用同一个。这个密码的作用是阻止拿着手机的人给自己加时间，它是「限额」和「建议」之间的分界线。

Apple 会提供用 Apple ID 找回的选项，选上它。以前忘了屏幕使用时间密码意味着整台设备要抹掉，现在这条恢复路径就是为这件事准备的。

今晚就做一件事

十分钟够了。给睡前两小时设停用时间，把地图、钱包、验证码 App 和电话加进「始终允许」，再给「社交」分类设一个限额。剩下的一周内都别动。

Apple ships a full set of screen time controls on every iPhone and almost nobody uses more than two of them. The reason is not laziness. The settings are spread across four menus, three of them use wording that does not match what the control does, and the one people look for first (a hard block) sits behind a passcode screen.

This is an ios screen time guide for the version shipping in 2026, written in the order you would actually set it up. Where a setting has a name that misleads, I say so.

Where the controls live

Open Settings, then Screen Time. Everything below hangs off that one screen, and it is worth knowing the four buckets before you tap anything:

SettingWhat it really doesWorth setting up?
DowntimeBlocks everything except the apps you whitelist, on a scheduleYes, the strongest one of the four
App LimitsCaps a category or a single app per dayYes, but start with categories
Communication LimitsControls who can reach you during downtimeOnly if you use Downtime
Content and PrivacyAge ratings, purchase blocks, web filteringMostly for a child's device

Downtime, and the whitelist nobody finds

Downtime is the setting that behaves closest to what people expect from screen time. Between the hours you set, every app goes grey except the ones you have explicitly allowed.

The part most guides skip is where the whitelist lives. It is not on the Downtime screen. Go to Screen Time, then Always Allowed, and pick the apps that should stay reachable. Do this before you set the hours. The first evening of downtime without Maps, Wallet or your authenticator app is unpleasant enough that most people turn the whole feature off and never come back to it.

One more, if the phone belongs to a child: set downtime under Family Sharing rather than on the device. A downtime schedule set on the phone can be approved away by the person holding it, which is the entire reason the separate setting exists.

App Limits, and why categories beat single apps

App Limits is where people overreach. They set a thirty minute cap on one social app, hit it by two in the afternoon, and tap Ignore Limit for the rest of the week. Once a limit has been ignored three times, the number stops meaning anything.

Categories work better for a first pass. One limit across Social instead of four separate limits per app means you decide how much scrolling you want, and the phone does not care which app you spend it on. That is closer to how the habit behaves.

The Block at End of Limit toggle is the one that matters. With it off, hitting the limit shows a grey screen with a button that lets you carry on. With it on, the app stops until the next day or until somebody enters the passcode.

Three settings most guides skip

  • Screen Distance. Under Screen Time there is a setting that uses the TrueDepth camera to notice when the phone has been held closer than about 30cm for a while. It reads like a gimmick, and it is the only control on the phone that addresses the posture problem rather than the hours problem.
  • Notification delivery timing. Most setup guides cover notification counts and stop there. The larger lever is when notifications arrive, and it is set per app rather than globally, which is why nobody finds it.
  • Share Across Devices. It is on by default, which means an iPad or a Mac counts against the same limits. Useful when you want one number. Turn it off if a work iPad is inflating the total on your personal phone.

Where the passcode fits

Set a Screen Time passcode, and do not reuse the device passcode. The passcode is what stops the person holding the phone from granting themselves more time, and it is the difference between a limit and a suggestion.

Apple offers to let you recover it with the Apple ID on the device. Take that option. A forgotten Screen Time passcode used to mean wiping the phone, and the recovery path exists for exactly that reason.

Start tonight

Ten minutes is enough. Set Downtime for the two hours before bed, add Maps, Wallet, your authenticator and Phone to Always Allowed, then set one category limit on Social. Leave everything else alone for a week.
