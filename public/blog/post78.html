Doom Scrolling 与抑郁：恶性循环

2026-10-24

这个循环怎么形成、什么会放大它，以及最小的一次打断。讲的是习惯，不是医学。

Doom Scrolling 与抑郁 这个词，多数人是在解释一种没来由的低落时才用上的。那天没出什么坏事：新闻很糟，信息流一直送来很糟的新闻，到了半夜，整个晚上就都废了。这篇讲这个循环怎么形成、什么会放大它，以及最小的一次打断。

先把边界说清楚。我写的是习惯，不是医学。这里没有任何东西能治疗抑郁；如果低落持续了几周而不是一个糟糕的晚上，下一步是找专业人员聊，不是装一个浏览器插件。Doom Scrolling 值得修，但它肯定不是全部。

循环是怎么形成的

整件事分四个动作，衔接快到看不出接缝。

动作长什么样可以在哪里打断
拿起手机已经在手里了，中间没有任何决定拉开距离：充电器放到别的房间
打开靠肌肉记忆打开的 feed，不是选择的结果把它从第一屏挪走
坏消息一条标题足够让人不安，逼着你去看下一条加载之前停十秒
走到头两个小时没了，心情比开始时更低固定时间硬性收尾

第三个动作是发动机。feed 按互动量排序，而坏消息在这个指标上天然占优。没有人故意设计让人难受，排序自己就会走到那一步。

doom scrolling 心理健康：什么会放大它

三个放大器，能把偶尔一次的坏滚动，变成每晚都来一次。

  • 时间点。留在记忆里的那次几乎总是发生在晚上十点以后，没有其他东西跟你抢注意力，明天又显得很远。
  • 躺着刷。躺平去掉了「坐起来」这点摩擦，而摩擦正是多数习惯停下来的原因。
  • 等更新。正在发展的事没有结尾，没结尾的东西比读过的东西更抓人。

这三样都不会造成临床抑郁。它们做的是拿一个普通低落，不断往里加料，直到它长出牙齿。

刷屏和心情的分界线在哪

读远处陌生人的坏消息，和自己这一周里正在发生的事，带来的低落不是同一种。两者都会把情绪压下去，但通常只有后者还配着一件你能做的事。什么都做不了这一点，把一个沉重的晚上变成一个沉重的一周。

这里有两个小习惯，都跟信息无关。第一条：读一篇长文章，而不是三十条标题，因为长文章往往会给出一个结论，而 feed 永远不给。第二条：把新闻里你能真正动手的那一件事写下来，多数人这份清单很短，有时候是空的。空清单也是有用的信息，它告诉你剩下那些滚动不算调研。

为什么刷起来像在工作

这是把人留在循环里的那部分：读坏消息感觉像负责任。你在保持知情，你在为一个问题做点什么，哪怕这个「做」指的全是读它。

这种感觉值得单独提一句，因为正是它让常见的建议全部失效。被人说「把手机放下」，等于被要求停止一件感觉像义务的事。换个说法更准：读一个问题，和对一个问题做出反应，是两件事，只有第二件会改变什么。

最小的一次打断

选一个，不要三个一起上。

  • 充电器放到厨房。晚上十一点，距离比意志力管用，代价只是难受两晚。
  • 固定时间收尾。定 22:30。到点之后，PausePaw 会让第一条 feed 等十秒才加载。十秒通常够想起来你已经困了。
  • 换姿势，不换 App。坐起来挪到椅子上，很多时候不用改任何设置就能收住。

十秒暂停是我建议先试的那个。它不屏蔽任何东西，所以半夜没什么可与它争论的，选择仍然在你手上。

低落已经来的时候

如果你是在半夜、带着那股情绪读到这里的，跳过所有策略。放下手机，喝口水，去睡。改习惯的时间是明天早上，不是半夜。

今晚只改一处

知道这四个动作并不会让它们松开，而且一次只能松开一个。充电器、固定时间、椅子，选一项，坚持一周。

Doom scrolling depression is a phrase people reach for when a bad mood arrives with nothing to point at. Nothing went wrong that day. The headlines were grim, the feed kept producing them, and by midnight the whole evening feels spent. This piece covers how that loop forms, what amplifies it, and the smallest break that interrupts it.

One caveat first. I write about habits, not medicine. Nothing here treats depression, and if low mood has been running for weeks rather than one bad evening, the next step is a conversation with a professional. Doom scrolling is worth fixing either way, and it is still not the whole picture.

How the loop forms

The cycle runs through four moves, and they follow each other fast enough that the joins are invisible.

MoveWhat it looks likeWhere it can be broken
Pick upThe phone is in your hand before any intention formedDistance: charger in another room
OpenThe feed app opens by muscle memory rather than choiceMove it off the first screen
Bad newsOne headline is alarming enough to demand the next oneA pause before the page loads
Dead endTwo hours gone, mood lower than where it startedA hard stop at a fixed hour

The third move is the engine. Feeds are ranked by engagement, and bad news scores well on that metric. Nobody has to design for misery. The ranking arrives there by itself.

Doom scrolling mental health: what amplifies the loop

Three amplifiers turn an occasional bad scroll into a nightly one.

  • Time of day. The session you remember almost always starts after 22:00, when nothing else competes for attention and tomorrow still feels abstract.
  • Lying down. Horizontal scrolling removes the small friction of sitting up, and friction carries most of the work of stopping a habit.
  • Refreshing for updates. A developing story has no ending, and unfinished items pull harder than anything already read.

None of these cause clinical depression. What they do is take an ordinary low mood and keep feeding it material until it grows teeth.

Where scrolling and mood part company

Reading bad news produced by people far away is not the same kind of sad as something happening in your own week. Both lower your mood. Only the second one usually comes with something you can do, and having nothing to do is what turns a heavy evening into a heavy week.

Two small habits help here, and neither involves information. Read one long piece instead of thirty headlines, because long writing tends to end with a conclusion while a feed never does. Then write down the one thing in the news that you could actually act on, which for most people is a short list, sometimes an empty one. An empty list is useful information too: it tells you the rest of the scroll is not research.

Why the scroll feels like work

This is the part that holds people in the cycle: reading bad news feels responsible. You are staying informed, you are doing something about a problem, even when the doing consists entirely of reading.

That feeling deserves naming, because it is why the usual advice fails. Being told to put the phone down asks you to stop something that feels like a duty. A more accurate framing: reading about a problem and responding to it are separate acts, and only the second one changes anything.

The smallest break that interrupts it

Choose one, not all three.

  • Charger in the kitchen. At 23:00, distance beats willpower, and it costs two awkward evenings.
  • A fixed closing hour. Pick 22:30. After that, PausePaw makes you wait ten seconds before the first feed loads. Ten seconds is often long enough to remember you were already tired.
  • Change position instead of app. Sitting up and moving to a chair frequently stops the session without touching a single setting.

The ten second pause is the one I would start with. It blocks nothing, so there is nothing to argue with at midnight, and the decision stays yours.

When the mood has already landed

If you are reading this at midnight with the feeling already present, skip the strategy. Put the phone down, drink some water, go to sleep. Tomorrow morning is a better time to change a habit than the middle of the night.

One change tonight

Knowing about the four moves does not loosen them, and only one comes loose at a time. Charger, fixed hour, or chair. Pick one and keep it for a week.
